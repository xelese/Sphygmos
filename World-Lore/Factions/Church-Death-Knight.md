### Who are they?

[Dead PC] is a former Tower surveyor

They walked into Verdant Stasis regions when most people still thought they were cursed ground. They cataloged the silence, the muted air, the preserved dead. They watched settlements evacuate and then watched the Tower fail to stabilize what was left.

They did this three times.

After the third region slipped from Verdant Stasis into full Collapse, [Dead PC] stopped filing reports. They walked south. They spent time among refugees and folk communities that had already begun living as though the world was ending — reducing draw, abandoning magic, mourning what they used to be.

When the ancient ruins were uncovered and the Church of the Last Descent formed around their implications, [Dead PC] did not need to be converted. They had already arrived at the same conclusion through fieldwork.

They volunteered for the sacrament within months.

---

### What They Believe

 [Dead PC] does not hate the Tower. They pity it.

They believe the Tower's researchers are brilliant, rigorous, and fundamentally mistaken. Stabilization delays the Descent. Every survey that succeeds is a tourniquet on a patient who needs surgery. Every field anchor placed is another nail holding a stressed plate together past the point where it could have failed cleanly and begun recovering.

They do not think the Tower is evil. They think it is afraid, and that its fear is killing the world slowly instead of letting it die quickly enough to heal.

Their own binding proves their conviction. They severed their coupling willingly. They draw nothing from the biosphere now. They are proof — walking, speaking, slowly diminishing proof — that letting go of the field is survivable, meaningful, and necessary.

---

### What They Want

- The Tower to stop stabilizing regions that are past saving.
- Weakened zones to be allowed to Collapse naturally.
- The ancient ruins preserved as evidence, not plundered for engineering.
- Their remaining time spent on actions that matter: disrupting Tower infrastructure, protecting Church communities, and forcing the world to confront the question it has been avoiding.

### What They Do Not Want

- Civilian massacre. [Dead PC] is not a butcher. They target infrastructure, equipment, and institutional authority.
- Worship. They dislike being treated as a saint or a relic.
- Pity. They chose this. They are clear on that point.

---

### Tension

[Dead PC] is dangerous precisely because they are not a fanatic.

They argue calmly. They concede points. They acknowledge the Tower's competence and the sincerity of its mission. They simply believe it is wrong, and they have given up their life to act on that belief.

A fanatic can be dismissed. [Dead PC] cannot. They have seen the same data the party has seen. They have walked the same dead ground. They reached the opposite conclusion through experience rather than ideology.

Their strongest argument is the one the party may not be able to answer:

> *"You have measured the field. You have documented the failure. You know how many regions were stable when your work began and how many are stable now. Tell me — honestly — is the gap closing? Or are you just counting the losses more carefully than anyone did before?"*

---

### Voice Notes

[Dead PC] speaks the way a careful scholar writes: measured, exact, aware of every clause. They do not raise their voice. They do not use threats. They treat conversation as fieldwork — they are collecting data on what the other person believes and offering their own observations in return.

They occasionally pause mid-sentence for slightly too long, then finish the thought without acknowledging the gap. This happens more frequently as their reservoir depletes.

When they are moved — which is rare — their voice does not crack. It simply gets quieter, as though the apparatus preserving them allocates less power to volume when something real reaches through.

They call Tower personnel by their titles. They call the party by their names if they have learned them. They never use insults.

**Sample lines:**

> *"You're surveyors. Good. Then you've seen what I've seen. You've stood in a place that used to answer and felt the silence. The only question between us is what the silence means."*

> *"I don't blame the Tower. I was the Tower, for eleven years. I filed the same reports you're filing. I believed the same things you believe. I stopped because the evidence stopped supporting them."*

> *"I don't breathe unless I'm speaking. I don't eat. I don't heal. I draw nothing from the field. If the world descends tomorrow, I am the only one at this table who costs it nothing."*

> *"You think I want the world to end. I want the world to begin again. Those are not the same thing."*

---

**Key traits:**

- Immune to Metabolic Drift and Collapse-zone environmental effects.
- Does not need to breathe, eat, drink, or rest.
- Less dependent on active spellcasting than a living caster. Counterspell and Dispel Magic are usually poor answers to them, but relic-core disruption and antimagic pressure can still interfere with their continuity.
- Melee-focused. Fights with a heavy longsword and tower shield. No flashy magic.
- Their attacks carry a faint field-drain effect: on a critical hit, the target feels a brief pulse of Vital Current being redirected into the relic core, as if the sword is sipping from the wound.
- As their reservoir depletes, their speed drops, their damage dims, and their voice thins. A long fight is a losing fight for them, and they know it. They fight to accomplish objectives, not to prove dominance.

**Retreat behavior:** [Dead PC] withdraws when the objective is achieved or when continuing would cost more reservoir than the outcome is worth. They do not fight to the death unless the stakes demand it. They are spent, not reckless.

---

### Campaign Role

 [Dead PC] should enter the campaign after the ancient ruins have been discovered and the Church of the Last Descent has formed.

They serve several functions:

- **Ideological mirror.** They ask the questions the party may already be asking themselves.
- **Moral complication.** They are not a villain who needs to be defeated. They are a person who needs to be answered.
- **Escalation vector.** When [Dead PC] acts, regions fall. Not because they are powerful, but because they target the infrastructure that keeps stressed zones above their tipping point.
- **Tragic endpoint.** If the campaign runs long enough, the party may watch them slow, thin, and finally stop. Not in battle. Just between sentences. The pause that never ends.
