# Subtle Skill Check

Idea inspiration [This Rule INSTANTLY Makes D&D More IMMERSIVE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-K7lXvGbc_A)

## What does it mean?

> 'Subtle Skill Check' are reserved for any check where the character's success or failure isn't immediately obvious to the characters.

Seeing a natural 1 on skill checks such as (insight or history) pulls players out of the story and forces them to act on dice knowledge instead of character knowledge. This mechanic adds a layer of complexity to only see the results and ignore the dice knowledge completely.

> **Example**
>
> Medicine is a Subtle Skill Check, unless you have proficiency in medicine. If you have medical training, you know when you've made a horrible mistake like a natural 1, where an untrained hand may not realize that they're actually making the problem worse.
>
> In certain situations, attacks can even be a Subtle Skill Check. If a player fires an arrow into a fog cloud where three goblins are hiding, that may be a Subtle Skill Check if there's no way they'd  know if, or even who, their arrow hit. Then later, you get this cool reveal where the fog clears and two goblins lie dead on the ground, arrows sticking from their bodies.

Moments like that can change the way your game feels at the table so don't lie to them about what the rolls actually are. If someone rolls a two, just let them not remember that obscure religious detail. ***Save moments of misinformation for real natural 1s only***. Don't invent critical fails for your own narrative advancement.

## Mechanics

Ask ***Why should the check be a Subtle Skill Check?***. Be open to their argument if they believe that their character would recognize a failure.


This can apply to any check without proficiency, however these seem the most appropriate:
- Arcana 
- History 
- Religion 
- Deception 
- Insight
- Investigation
- Perception
- Stealth
- Medicine


If the party fails a check and someone wants to make the same check again, the GM can propose a Subtle Skill Check as a last resort that immediately resolves the challenge (either good or bad) narratively without being able to take any further actions.
- If the character would not know they were wrong yet, make it Subtle.
- If the world answers immediately (crowd boos, character falls, takes damage), keep it normal.
