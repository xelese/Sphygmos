# Onboarding


Welcome! You do not need to know D&D already. We’ll guide you through it.

At our table, we use D&D Beyond for your character sheet and most mechanics, and Roll20 for maps, tokens, and visuals. 
Your main job is simple: describe what your character tries to do, and we’ll help with the rules. This game uses the 2024 D&D rules.

## What to have open

Keep these open during play:

- D&D Beyond: your character sheet
- Roll20: the map and handouts
- Discord Voice/chat with the group

## The basic loop


Most of play is this:


The DM describes the situation Where you are, what you see, what’s happening, and what feels dangerous or important. You say what your character does and add some "flair"

> Examples:
>
> “I ask the guard what happened.”
>
> “I search the desk.”
>
> “I sneak to the doorway.”
>
> “I attack the monster.”
>
> “I try to calm everyone down.”


You don’t need to act. You can speak in character, or just describe intent.


> Both are fine:
>
> I tell the noble "I don’t trust you”
>
> “My character tries to be polite and get more information.”


Sometimes you roll If the outcome is uncertain, we’ll ask for a roll. Usually that means rolling a d20 and adding a bonus from your sheet. 
DndBeyond bakes this complexity so all you have to do is click a button.


The story changes Success moves things forward. Failure still moves things forward, just usually with a cost, complication, or surprise.

## What matters on your character sheet

You do not need to memorize the whole sheet. 


> Focus on these:
>
> **Hit Points (HP)**
> How much damage you can take before going down.
>
> **Armor Class (AC)**
> How hard you are to hit.
>
> **Speed**
> How far you can move in combat.
>
> **Attack / Spell**
> Your main actions in a fight.
>
> **Ability**
> Used for things like sneaking, persuading, investigating, perceiving, and recalling knowledge.

### The three most common rolls

**Ability checks**
Used when you try something risky or uncertain.
Example: sneaking, climbing, persuading, searching.


**Attack rolls**
Used when you try to hit an enemy.


**Saving throws**
Used when something bad happens to you and you resist it.
Example: dodging a blast, resisting poison, fighting off fear.

## Combat

When a fight starts, we take turns by running something called the initiative order. 
Everyone rolls and we take turns in descending order. This includes friends and enemies alike.

> On your turn, you will usually do:
>
> 1. Move
> 2. One action
> 3. Sometimes a bonus action
> 4. Sometimes a reaction outside your turn

> combat often looks like this:
>
> 1. Roll d20 and add your attack modifier to see if you hit.
> 2. If you hit, roll damage.

If you’re unsure, say: “What are my best options here?”


### Teamwork wins games

New players often think they need to be clever alone. Usually the best move is to:
- help an ally,
- ask questions,
- protect someone weaker,
- or create an opening for the group.

### What beginners should do a lot


> Do not ask, “What should I roll?” first.

Say what your character is trying to do, and the DM will tell you the roll. That keeps the game feeling like a story instead of a math test.

- Ask questions
- Say what you want to accomplish
- Tell us your goal before worrying about rules
- Read your top 3 attacks/spells before combat starts
- Stay near the group unless you enjoy danger

Good play is about engaging with the world.

> Examples
>
> **Exploration:**
>
> “I check the room for anything unusual.”
>
> That might lead to Perception or Investigation.
>
> **Social**
>
> “I want to convince the guard we belong here.”
>
> That might lead to Persuasion or Deception.
>
> **Combat**
>
> “I move behind cover and shoot the cultist.”
>
> That uses movement plus an attack.
>
> **Creative**
>
> “I tip the brazier to create a wall of fire between us and them.”
>
> That’s exactly the kind of idea you should bring up.

## Dice Rolling: What You Actually Roll

Most of the time, you roll a 20 sided die often referred to as d20. When you try something risky or uncertain, roll:


d20 + the bonus on your character sheet then tell the DM the total.


D&D Beyond bakes this complexity so all you have to do is click the button and tell DM what you rolled + bonus.

### What the other dice do

The d20 decides whether you succeed. Other dice usually decide how much happens.

> Examples:
>
> 1d8 damage from a weapon
>
> 1d6 healing from a spell
>
> 2d6 damage from an effect

### Advantage and disadvantage

Sometimes you roll two d20s.

Advantage = roll 2 d20, keep the higher one

Disadvantage = roll 2 d20, keep the lower one

### Natural 20 and natural 1

If the d20 itself shows:

- 20 = a natural 20
- 1 = a natural 1

For attacks, a natural 20 is critical success. Most of the time it means success with bonus.
A natural 1 a critical miss. Often it's a failure with really negative outcome.

## Campaign-specific expectations

This campaign uses a few table rules that matter a lot in play. You do not need to memorize them, but you should know they exist so they do not feel like surprises.

### Resting is not automatic

In many D&D games, the group can stop for the night and expect a normal Long Rest.


In this campaign, that is only reliably true in places that are actually safe, such as:

- towns and defended settlements
- secure inns or safe houses
- strong refuges the party has earned


Out in the field, rest depends on:

- whether you brought food and clean water
- how dangerous the current region is
- how well the group sets up camp

That means camp preparation matters. Where you sleep, who keeps watch, whether you have shelter, and how you protect supplies can change the result.

#### What this means for you as a player

- Bring rations and protect your water.
- Ask whether the area feels safe enough to rest.
- Treat camp setup as part of play, not as a skipped detail.
- Do not assume every night resets all your resources.

If the group rests in dangerous territory, you may get a weaker version of a Long Rest or only the benefits of a Short Rest.

#### The GM will ask questions, not just say yes or no

When the party beds down, expect questions like:

- Where are you sleeping?
- Who is taking watch?
- How are you staying warm, dry, and hidden?
- What gear, magic, or precautions are you using?

Those questions are there to reward planning, not to trap you.

> **The world gets harder as collapse spreads**
>
> Some regions are harder to recover in than others.
>
> Safe rest becomes rarer in the wilderness. Some regions are especially hostile to recovery even when no monster is attacking you.

### Bastion System

The High Tower is the party's bastion. It starts as a basic command center and expands as survey teams collect **Science Points** — earned by successfully using scientific equipment in the field.


As the Tower levels up between surveys, new wings are built, new facilities open, and better recruits arrive. Benefits from a level-up apply starting from the **next** survey, not the one that earned the points.


The party also manages a shared **Surveyor Roster**: a pool of characters that grows as the Tower advances. Before each survey, each player picks one character from the roster to deploy. Others stay at the Tower and train between missions. If a character dies or retires, the slot opens for someone new.


### Subtle Checks

This campaign uses a Subtle Skill Check idea for some checks where your character would not immediately know whether they succeeded or failed. 


That means you may not always get clear “I rolled bad, so I failed” feedback in the moment. 


It’s there to keep immersion high and avoid players acting on dice knowledge instead of character knowledge.
